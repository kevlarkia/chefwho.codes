Glossary of Key Terms

  • ABI (Ankle-Brachial Index): A test that compares blood pressure in the ankle to blood pressure in the arm to check for peripheral artery disease (PAD).
  • Atherectomy: A minimally invasive procedure that removes plaque buildup inside an artery.
  • Angioplasty: A procedure to open blocked or narrowed blood vessels. Often involves inserting a balloon catheter to widen the vessel.
  • DCB (Drug-Coated Balloon): A balloon used in angioplasty that is coated with medication to help prevent the artery from narrowing again.
  • Claudication: Pain, ache, or cramp in the legs that occurs during exercise and is relieved by rest, often a symptom of peripheral artery disease.
  • Ischemia: A condition in which blood flow (and thus oxygen) is restricted or reduced in a part of the body.
  • Digital Ischemia: Reduced blood flow to the fingers or toes.
  • Biphasic/Triphasic: Terms used to describe the waveform pattern of blood flow in arteries, as measured by Doppler ultrasound. Triphasic is generally considered normal, while biphasic or monophasic can indicate arterial disease.
  • Monophasic: A waveform pattern of blood flow that indicates a single phase, often suggesting arterial obstruction.
  • TBI (Toe-Brachial Index): Similar to ABI, but measures blood pressure in the toe, used when ankle pressures are unreliable.
  • Peripheral Vascular Disease (PVD): A condition affecting blood vessels outside the heart and brain, commonly affecting the legs and feet.
  • COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease): A chronic inflammatory lung disease that obstructs airflow from the lungs.
  • HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein): Often called "good" cholesterol, it helps remove other forms of cholesterol from the bloodstream.
  • Atherosclerosis: The buildup of fats, cholesterol, and other substances in and on the artery walls.
  • Spondylosis: Degeneration of the spine.
  • Macrocyotsis: Condition in which red blood cells are larger than normal.
  • Fibromuscular Dysplasia (FMD): A condition that causes narrowing (stenosis), bulging (aneurysm), or tearing (dissection) in the walls of your arteries.
  • PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9): A brief questionnaire used to screen for and monitor the severity of depression.
  • D-dimer: A protein fragment that is produced when a blood clot dissolves in the body. Elevated levels can indicate the presence of a significant blood clot.
  • UA (Urinalysis): A series of tests performed on urine to detect and measure various compounds that pass through the urine. It is used to detect urinary tract infections, kidney disease, and other medical conditions.
  • FEU (Fibrinogen Equivalent Units): A unit of measurement used for D-dimer tests, which can influence the numerical value of the result.
  • HFAA (Hydrofluoroalkane): A propellant used in inhalers.
