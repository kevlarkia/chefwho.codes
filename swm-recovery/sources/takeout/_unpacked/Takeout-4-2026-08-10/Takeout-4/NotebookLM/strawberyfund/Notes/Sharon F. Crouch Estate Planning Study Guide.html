III. Glossary of Key Terms

  • Advance Health Care Directive: A legal document that allows an individual to specify their healthcare wishes and appoint someone to make medical decisions on their behalf if they become incapacitated.
  • Durable Power of Attorney: A legal document that grants an agent broad powers to manage the principal's financial affairs and personal property, even in the event of the principal's incapacity.
  • Living Trust (Revocable): A legal arrangement established during a person's lifetime, where assets are placed in a trust for their benefit, with provisions for distribution to beneficiaries upon their death. The trust can be modified or revoked by the grantor during their lifetime.
  • Trustee: An individual or entity responsible for managing the assets held within a trust, according to the terms of the trust agreement.
  • Beneficiary: A person or entity who will receive benefits from a trust, will, or other legal arrangement.
  • Grant Deed: A legal document used to transfer ownership of real property from one party (grantor) to another (grantee).
  • HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act): U.S. legislation that provides data privacy and security provisions for safeguarding medical information.
  • Conservator: A person appointed by a court to manage the financial affairs and/or daily life of another person who is deemed incapable of managing themself.
  • Executor: The person named in a will to carry out the testator's wishes regarding the distribution of their estate after death.
  • No-Contest Provision: A clause in a will or trust that penalizes beneficiaries who challenge the validity of the document by forfeiting their inheritance.
  • Testator: The person making the will.
  • Grantor: A person who transfers assets into a trust, also known as the trustor or settlor.
  • Agent: A person authorized to act on behalf of another (the principal), either for healthcare or financial matters.
  • Incapacity: The state of being unable to make decisions or manage one's affairs, often due to illness, injury, or cognitive decline.
  • Special Needs Trust: A specific type of trust designed to benefit a person with disabilities without jeopardizing their eligibility for government benefits such as Medicaid or Supplemental Security Income (SSI).
  • U/A: An abbreviation for "under agreement." It references the date of a trust agreement, and it can appear in property titles, for example, "SHARON F. CROUCH, as Trustee of THE SHARON F. CROUCH LIVING TRUST, U/A dated December 2, 2019."
  • Fiduciary: A person who is responsible for managing the assets of another person or a group of people. Among other things, fiduciaries owe a duty of care and a duty of loyalty to the people whose assets they manage.
