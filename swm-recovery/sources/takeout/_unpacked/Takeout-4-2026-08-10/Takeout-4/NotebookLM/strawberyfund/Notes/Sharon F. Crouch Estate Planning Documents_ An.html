Frequently Asked Questions about Sharon F. Crouch's Estate Planning Documents

  • What is the purpose of the Sharon F. Crouch Living Trust?

    The Sharon F. Crouch Living Trust is a revocable trust created to manage Sharon F. Crouch's assets during her lifetime and to distribute them to her beneficiaries after her death, potentially avoiding probate. It allows for the management of assets by Sharon F. Crouch as the initial Trustee, and designates successor Trustees (Frank C. Fernandez, then Cory M. Reil) to manage the Trust if she is unable to do so.

  • What is the significance of the Durable Power of Attorney for Management of Property and Personal Affairs?

    The Durable Power of Attorney (DPOA) grants Frank C. Fernandez, as the primary agent, broad powers to manage Sharon F. Crouch's property and personal affairs, including financial transactions, digital assets, and government benefits, in the event she becomes incapacitated. Cory M. Reil serves as the alternate agent. The DPOA takes effect upon Sharon's incapacity.

  • How does the Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD) work?

    The AHCD allows Sharon F. Crouch to appoint Frank C. Fernandez as her health care agent (with Cory M. Reil as the alternate) to make medical decisions on her behalf if she is unable to do so herself. It also contains instructions regarding her wishes for end-of-life care, including preferences regarding life-sustaining treatment, pain relief, and anatomical gifts.

  • What provisions are included in the Advance Health Care Directive regarding end-of-life care?

    The AHCD expresses Sharon F. Crouch's wishes regarding medical care in situations where she is in a persistent vegetative state, irreversible coma, or terminal condition. She directs that treatment for pain or discomfort should always be provided. She states preferences to refuse treatments that prolong life artificially and expresses wishes concerning cardio-pulmonary resuscitation, surgery, and radiation therapy based on her medical condition.

  • What happens to Sharon F. Crouch's real property based on these documents?

    The Grant Deed to a Revocable Trust transfers Sharon F. Crouch's real property, specifically the property located at 1787 Jennifer Drive, Livermore, CA 94550, to the Sharon F. Crouch Living Trust. This allows the property to be managed and distributed according to the terms of the Trust, potentially bypassing probate upon her death.

  • What are some of the specific powers granted to the Trustee of the Living Trust?

    The Trustee has broad powers to manage and control the Trust's assets, including the power to buy, sell, hold, transfer, and convey assets; maintain bank and brokerage accounts; purchase insurance; borrow money; and exercise rights in digital assets and electronic communications. The Trustee is also empowered to act as an Attorney-in-Fact.

  • Who are the beneficiaries of Sharon F. Crouch's Living Trust, and how will the assets be distributed after her death?

    After Sharon F. Crouch's death, the Trust specifies the distribution of assets, including specific instructions for tangible personal property and the distribution of the remainder of the Trust Estate. Frank C. Fernandez will benefit from a Special Needs Trust, and other beneficiaries include Cory M. Reil, and grandchildren, Anthony R. Reil Jr. and Casey G. Reil.

  • What is the purpose of Sharon F. Crouch's Last Will and Testament?

    The Last Will and Testament (Will) serves to nominate Frank C. Fernandez (and Cory M. Reil as successor) as executor of Sharon F. Crouch's estate. It directs the payment of estate expenses and debts. Importantly, the Will designates that the residuary estate is to be distributed to the already established Living Trust. It also grants the executor powers over digital assets and addresses tax elections. The will includes a no-contest provision.
