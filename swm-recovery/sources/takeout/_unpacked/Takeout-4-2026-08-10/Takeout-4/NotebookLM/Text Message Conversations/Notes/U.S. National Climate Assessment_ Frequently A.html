Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the primary focus of the U.S. National Climate Assessment (NCA), and who is its intended audience?

The U.S. National Climate Assessment (NCA) serves as a comprehensive and authoritative synthesis of scientific information regarding the impacts of climate change on the United States. Its primary focus is to evaluate the observed and projected consequences of global warming on various sectors, regions, and communities across the nation, spanning human health, agriculture, energy, water resources, ecosystems, and more. The intended audience is broad, encompassing policymakers at all levels of government, business leaders, community organizations, educators, researchers, and the general public. By providing clear and accessible information, the NCA aims to inform decision-making, promote climate literacy, and support efforts to enhance resilience and mitigate future risks.

2. How does the NCA utilize scientific evidence to reach its conclusions about climate change impacts?

The NCA relies on a rigorous and transparent process that integrates findings from a wide range of scientific disciplines. It synthesizes information from peer-reviewed research, observational data, climate models, and technical reports. Expert authors, drawn from academia, government agencies, and the private sector, analyze this evidence to assess current and future climate change impacts. The assessment process includes multiple stages of review, including public and expert input, to ensure the accuracy and robustness of its findings. This comprehensive approach allows the NCA to present a well-supported understanding of climate change and its consequences.

3. What are some of the key observed impacts of climate change highlighted in the NCA?

The NCA documents a multitude of observed impacts across the United States. These include rising average temperatures, more frequent and intense heat waves, changes in precipitation patterns leading to both increased flooding and drought in different regions, more powerful hurricanes and other extreme weather events, sea level rise and coastal erosion, ocean acidification, shifts in plant and animal ranges and behavior, and increasing risks to human health, such as heatstroke and respiratory illnesses. These observed impacts are already affecting communities, infrastructure, and natural resources nationwide.

4. What are some of the major projected future impacts of climate change for the U.S., according to the NCA?

The NCA projects that many of the observed trends will continue and intensify in the future. Depending on the rate of greenhouse gas emissions, the U.S. can expect further increases in average temperatures, more severe extreme weather events, continued sea level rise threatening coastal communities, disruptions to agricultural production, increased risks to water supplies, greater stress on ecosystems and biodiversity, and significant challenges to human health and well-being. The assessment emphasizes that the magnitude of these future impacts will depend on actions taken to reduce emissions and adapt to a changing climate.

5. How does the NCA address the concept of vulnerability and environmental justice in the context of climate change?

The NCA explicitly recognizes that the impacts of climate change are not uniform and that certain populations and regions are disproportionately vulnerable. It highlights how factors such as socioeconomic status, race, ethnicity, age, and geographic location can influence exposure and sensitivity to climate hazards, as well as adaptive capacity. The assessment integrates considerations of environmental justice, emphasizing that marginalized communities often bear the brunt of climate change impacts due to historical inequities and systemic disadvantages. It calls for equitable solutions that address these disparities and promote resilience for all.

6. What role does the NCA play in informing climate action and decision-making?

The NCA serves as a critical resource for informing climate action and decision-making at various levels. By providing a comprehensive understanding of climate change impacts and risks, it helps policymakers develop informed strategies for mitigation (reducing greenhouse gas emissions) and adaptation (adjusting to the unavoidable changes). Businesses can use the NCA to assess climate-related risks to their operations and supply chains, while communities can utilize its findings to plan for and build resilience to local climate hazards. The NCA also supports public education and engagement on climate change issues.

7. How does the NCA address the uncertainties associated with climate projections?

While the NCA relies on the best available scientific evidence, it also acknowledges and addresses the inherent uncertainties in climate projections. It discusses the range of potential future climate scenarios based on different emissions pathways and the limitations of climate models. The assessment uses terms like "likely," "very likely," and "virtually certain" to communicate the level of confidence in its findings. By explicitly addressing uncertainties, the NCA aims to provide a nuanced understanding of future climate risks rather than presenting definitive predictions.

8. How frequently is the NCA updated, and why is this periodic assessment important?

The U.S. National Climate Assessment is intended to be a periodic assessment, typically produced every four to five years. This frequency is crucial because the scientific understanding of climate change is constantly evolving as new research emerges, data records lengthen, and climate models are improved. Regular updates ensure that the assessment reflects the most current state of knowledge and can continue to provide relevant and timely information for decision-making in a rapidly changing climate. The periodic nature also allows for the incorporation of observed impacts and the evaluation of the effectiveness of climate-related actions.
