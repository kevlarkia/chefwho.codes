The Fernandez Family Trust: A Comprehensive Study Guide

I. Key Concepts and Principles

This section outlines the fundamental aspects of the Fernandez Family Trust as detailed in the provided documents.

A. Trust Establishment and Purpose:

  • The document represents the Third Amended and Restated Fernandez Family Trust, initially created on June 8, 1994.
  • The Settlors (Husband and Wife) establish this inter vivos revocable trust under California law.
  • The primary purpose is to hold and manage their community and separate property for their benefit during their lifetimes.
  • Upon the death of the last surviving Settlor, the trust is intended to provide for their issue (currently two living children: Frank G. Fernandez, Jr., and James R. Fernandez).

B. Trust Assets (Trust Estate):

  • The trust estate encompasses all property transferred to the trust by the Settlors.
  • This includes community property, separate property (which retains its character upon transfer), and quasi-community property (treated as the transferring Settlor's separate property for convenience but retains its underlying character).
  • Joint tenancy property intended to be part of the trust becomes trust property upon transfer.

C. Trust Administration During Settlors' Joint Lives:

  • The Trustees (initially Husband and Wife) manage the trust assets.
  • Income from community property within the trust is typically paid to the Settlors or as they direct.

D. Trustee Powers and Responsibilities (Article X):

  • The Trustees are granted broad powers to manage and control the trust estate, acting as prudent persons with a focus on the Settlors' goals.
  • These powers include investment authority (including market funds, index funds, margin accounts, and options), the ability to retain or sell property, manage securities (voting, participating in agreements, consenting to corporate actions), acquire and dispose of property, lease (including mineral development), borrow money, initiate or defend litigation, and many other enumerated powers.
  • The Trustees have specific powers related to S corporation stock held within the trust to maintain its eligibility.
  • Trustees are generally liable for breaches of trust, but the instrument includes some limitations on non-liability, though these have exceptions.

E. Distribution Upon Death of Settlors:

  • The document outlines various distribution scenarios upon the death of one or both Settlors, including provisions for a Disclaimer Trust and the distribution of personal property.
  • There are specific clauses regarding the distribution of the Disclaimer Trust and the overall Trust Estate after the death of the last survivor.
  • The trust also includes provisions for the surviving spouse's ability to reside in the personal residence.

F. Revocation and Amendment (Article IX):

  • During their joint lifetimes, the Settlors generally have the power to amend or revoke the trust, with some limitations to avoid substantial alterations to estate distribution during a Settlor's lifetime or after death.

G. Important Clauses:

  • Spendthrift Clause: Prevents beneficiaries from anticipating, assigning, or encumbering their trust interests before receiving them, protecting assets from creditors.
  • Choice of Law: Specifies that California law governs the trust's validity, construction, interpretation, and administration, except for real property, which is governed by the laws of its location.
  • Construction of Terms: Provides rules for interpreting the language of the trust instrument (e.g., gender neutrality, the non-binding nature of headings).
  • Definitions: Clarifies the meaning of specific terms used in the trust document (e.g., "education," "issue," "independent person").
  • Perpetuities Savings Clause: Aims to prevent the trust from violating rules against perpetuities.
  • No Contest Clause: Discourages beneficiaries from challenging the trust.
  • Disclaimers: Allows beneficiaries to refuse to accept trust property.
  • Severability: Ensures that if any part of the trust is unenforceable, the rest remains valid.
  • Probate Court Jurisdiction Available: Indicates that the trust is subject to optional probate court jurisdiction under California law.

H. Successor Trustees (Article XIII):

  • Outlines the process for appointing successor trustees if the initial Trustees cease to act.

I. Accounting by Trustees (Article XIV):

  • Specifies the requirements for how Trustees must provide accountings to beneficiaries, including the form, content, and delivery method. It also details the process for beneficiaries to object to an accounting and the legal consequences of not doing so within the specified timeframe.

J. S Corporation Stock (Special Considerations):

  • The trust includes specific provisions for managing any S corporation stock to ensure the trust remains an eligible shareholder under federal tax law. This may involve creating separate share trusts.

K. Forensic Accountant Engagement:

  • A forensic accountant has been engaged by the Fernandez Family Trust Temporary Oversight Committee.
  • The scope of their services includes a forensic audit of financial management, review of investments and disbursements, verification of alignment with trust terms, attending meetings, preparing financial summaries and a final report, and collaborating with legal counsel.
  • The goals of this engagement are to verify financial accuracy, assess investment prudence, evaluate distribution fairness, identify conflicts of interest, ensure transparency, and assess adherence to legal and regulatory standards, ultimately protecting the beneficiaries' interests.

  • Correspondence from Frank Clinton Fernandez to Nicole Patterson indicates that legal counsel represents Nicole Patterson in her fiduciary capacity as Trustee.
  • It clarifies that communication with other interested parties does not establish an attorney-client relationship with them.

V. Glossary of Key Terms

  • Settlor (Grantor/Trustor): The person or persons who create a trust and transfer their property into it. In this case, the Husband and Wife.
  • Trustee: The individual or entity responsible for managing the trust assets according to the terms of the trust document and in the best interests of the beneficiaries.
  • Beneficiary: The person or persons who will ultimately benefit from the trust assets, either during the Settlors' lives or upon their death.
  • Trust Estate (Trust Property): The assets that are held within the trust, including real estate, financial accounts, and personal property.
  • Community Property: Property acquired by a husband and wife during their marriage while living in a community property state, other than by gift or inheritance.
  • Separate Property: Property owned by a person before marriage, or acquired during marriage by gift or inheritance, or acquired with separate funds.
  • Quasi-Community Property: Property acquired by a married couple while living in a common law state that would have been community property if they had been living in a community property state at the time of acquisition.
  • Joint Tenancy: A form of property ownership where two or more people hold equal shares of a property with the right of survivorship, meaning if one owner dies, their share automatically passes to the surviving owner(s).
  • Inter Vivos Trust (Living Trust): A trust created and funded by the Settlor during their lifetime.
  • Revocable Trust: A trust that the Settlor can amend, revoke, or terminate during their lifetime.
  • Irrevocable Trust: A trust that generally cannot be amended, revoked, or terminated once it is created.
  • Power of Appointment: A legal right given to a person (the donee) to designate who will receive certain property at a specified time.
  • Disclaimer Trust: A type of trust that may be created upon the death of the first spouse if the surviving spouse disclaims (refuses to accept) certain assets, potentially for tax planning purposes.
  • Pour-Over Will: A will that directs any assets owned by the deceased at the time of death to be added to a previously established trust.
  • Fiduciary Duty: A legal and ethical obligation to act in the best interests of another party (in this case, the Trustees to the beneficiaries).
  • Prudent Person Rule: A standard requiring trustees to invest and manage trust assets with the care, skill, prudence, and diligence that a prudent person acting in a similar capacity and familiar with such matters would use.
  • Spendthrift Clause: A provision in a trust that restricts the ability of beneficiaries to transfer their interest in the trust to others or to have it reached by their creditors before actual receipt.
  • Perpetuities Savings Clause: A provision intended to prevent a trust from violating the rule against perpetuities, which limits how long a trust can last.
  • No Contest Clause (In Terrorem Clause): A provision in a trust that discourages beneficiaries from challenging the validity of the trust by stipulating that if they do so and lose, they will forfeit their benefits under the trust.
  • Choice of Law Clause: A provision in a legal document that specifies which jurisdiction's laws will govern the interpretation and enforcement of the document.
  • Severability Clause: A provision stating that if any part of the document is found to be invalid or unenforceable, the remaining parts will still be in effect.
  • Probate: The legal process of administering the estate of a deceased person, typically involving proving the will, appointing an executor or administrator, identifying and valuing assets, paying debts and taxes, and distributing the remaining assets to the beneficiaries.
  • Successor Trustee: A person or entity designated in the trust document to take over the role of trustee if the original trustee is unable or unwilling to continue serving.
  • Accounting (Trust Accounting): A formal report prepared by the trustee detailing the financial activities of the trust over a specific period, including income, expenses, distributions, and asset valuations.
  • S Corporation: A type of corporation that meets specific Internal Revenue Code requirements, giving it the benefit of being taxed under Subchapter S of the Code. This allows income and losses to be passed through directly to the shareholders without being subject to corporate tax rates.
  • Qualified Subchapter S Trust (QSST): A type of trust that is permitted to hold stock in an S corporation, provided it meets certain requirements regarding income distribution and beneficiary rights.
  • Electing Small Business Trust (ESBT): Another type of trust that is permitted to hold stock in an S corporation, offering more flexibility than a QSST in terms of beneficiaries and income distribution.
  • Forensic Audit: An examination of a company's or individual's financial records to look for evidence of fraud, embezzlement, or other financial irregularities. In this context, it involves a detailed review of the trust's financial management.
