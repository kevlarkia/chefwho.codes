Executive Summary:

This briefing document summarizes the key aspects of the Fernandez Family Trust, as evidenced by the provided excerpts from the trust document and related materials. The trust is a California revocable living trust established by a Husband and Wife (Settlors) to manage their community and separate property during their lives and provide for their issue (two named children: Frank G. Fernandez, Jr., and James R. Fernandez) upon their death. The document outlines the creation of the trust, defines the trust estate, specifies distribution rules, grants broad powers to the Trustees, and includes various standard trust clauses. The engagement letter indicates the need for a forensic audit of the trust's financial management, suggesting potential concerns regarding the trustees' actions.

Main Themes and Important Ideas/Facts:

1. Trust Establishment and Purpose:

  • The document is the Third Amended and Restated Fernandez Family Trust, originally created on June 8, 1994, and restated on November 29, 2018, with a First Amendment on January 12, 2021.
  • The trust is an inter vivos (living) revocable trust established under California law. The Settlors intend to hold their community property and separate property in trust, manage it for their benefit during their lives, and provide for their children after their deaths.
  • The trust may be referred to collectively as "THE FERNANDEZ FAMILY TRUST," with individual trusts potentially named after beneficiaries.
  • The Settlors have two living children: Frank G. Fernandez, Jr. (born January 20, 1952) and James R. Fernandez (born October 6, 1953).

2. Trust Property:

  • The trust estate includes community property, separate property (which retains its character upon transfer to the trust - "the separate property of that Settlor shall retain its character as such notwithstanding its transfer to this trust"), and quasi-community property (treated as separate property within the trust for convenience but retains its underlying character).
  • Joint tenancy property transferred to the trust is also addressed.

3. Distribution Provisions:

  • The document outlines distribution rules during the Settlors' joint lives, including the payment of income from community property.
  • Article VIII addresses distribution upon the death of the Settlors, including provisions for general powers of appointment, pour-over to a disclaimer trust, distribution of personal property, and the distribution of the disclaimer trust and overall trust estate. However, the specific details of these distributions are not fully provided in the excerpts.

4. Trustee Powers:

  • Article X grants the Trustees very broad powers, stating they "shall have all the rights, powers, and privileges that an absolute owner of the same property would have, subject to the Trustees' fiduciary obligations and to any limitations stated elsewhere in this instrument."
  • Specific powers listed include:
    • Investment Powers: "Broad Investment Power Under Prudent Person Rule," power to invest in market funds and index funds, establish margin accounts, and buy/sell options. The Trustees are instructed to exercise "care, skill, and caution" considering individual investments as part of an overall strategy.
    • Power to Retain Property: Including their own stock and operate any acquired business. "The Trustees may, in the Trustees' discretion, continue to hold any property... for as long as the Trustees consider advisable."
    • Securities Management: Voting rights, participation in voting trusts, consent to corporate actions, depositing securities, and exercising stock rights.
    • Holding Securities: In their name as Trustees, a nominee's name, or in "street name." They can also hold unregistered securities in "bearer" form.
    • Acquiring, Disposing, and Managing Property: Broad authority to buy, sell, lease, develop, and encumber trust property. "The powers granted in this clause parallel and summarize those conferred in the California Probate Code and are not intended to limit the powers now or hereafter conferred by law."
    • Leasing: Including oil and mineral development.
    • Transactions with Settlor's Estate: Power to lend to and purchase property from the Settlor's probate estate.
    • Self-Dealing: Limited power to lend to, buy from, and sell to the trust individually, provided it's at fair market value and with interest at current rates for loans.
    • Borrowing: With the ability to encumber trust property, but with a restriction that assets of the disclaimer trust cannot be used to guarantee the surviving spouse's debts if they are the Trustee.
    • Litigation and Claims: Power to initiate and defend litigation and compromise claims.
    • Insurance and Regulatory Compliance: Power to insure assets and against liability, and to monitor property for regulatory compliance.
    • Digital Assets: Specific power regarding the management of the Settlors' digital assets.

5. Trust Administration Clauses:

  • Spendthrift Clause: "No interest in the income or principal of any trust created under this instrument shall be voluntarily or involuntarily anticipated, assigned, encumbered, or subjected to creditor's claim or legal process before actual receipt by the beneficiary."
  • Choice of Law: California law governs the trust, except for real property, which is governed by the laws of its location.
  • Construction of Terms: Provides rules for interpreting the trust document (e.g., gender neutrality, headings are not controlling, definitions of statutory references).
  • Definitions: Defines key terms used in the document, including "education," "adopted child," "independent person," and "Internal Revenue Code." Notably, "child," "children," and "issue" do not include stepchildren or non-legally adopted individuals unless specifically named.
  • Perpetuities Savings Clause: Included to prevent the trust from violating rules against perpetuities.
  • Disclaimers: Provisions regarding beneficiaries' ability to disclaim interests.
  • Accounting by Trustees: Specifies the form, content, and delivery of accountings and outlines the process for beneficiary objections, including a three-year limitation period based on Probate Code Section 16460.
  • Severability: If any part of the trust is unenforceable, the rest remains in effect.
  • Probate Court Jurisdiction: Explicitly states that Probate Code provisions authorizing optional probate court jurisdiction over revocable trusts apply.

6. S Corporation Stock:

  • The trust contains provisions addressing the holding of S corporation stock, requiring that any trust holding such stock be administered as a Qualified Subchapter S Trust (QSST) or an Electing Small Business Trust (ESBT) to maintain the S corporation status.
  • It mandates the creation of separate share trusts for each beneficiary holding S corporation stock and grants the Trustees powers to ensure these trusts remain eligible shareholders.

7. Forensic Accountant Engagement:

  • The Engagement Letter confirms the hiring of a Forensic Accountant for the Fernandez Family Trust Temporary Oversight Committee.
  • The scope of services includes a forensic audit of the trustees' financial management, review of financial documents, verification of alignment with trust terms, attending committee meetings, preparing financial updates and summaries, collaborating with the Trust Litigation Attorney, and preparing a final audit report.
  • The objectives of the engagement are to assess financial management, protect beneficiary interests (Frank and James Fernandez), and specifically to verify financial record accuracy, assess investment prudence, evaluate asset distribution fairness, identify conflicts of interest, and ensure transparency.
  • The audit scope covers financial records, investment decisions, and asset distribution.
  • The methodology includes document review, interviews (with trustees, beneficiaries, financial advisors, and legal counsel), financial analysis (ratio and trend analysis, benchmarking), compliance testing, and forensic techniques if necessary.

8. Potential Issues/Areas of Concern:

  • The engagement of a forensic accountant strongly suggests potential concerns regarding the current or past management of the trust by the trustees. The specific objectives outlined in the engagement letter (e.g., identifying conflicts of interest, breaches of fiduciary duty, assessing investment prudence) highlight the areas of scrutiny.
  • The notification by the trustee mentioned in one excerpt indicates that beneficiaries have received a copy of the trust document and a notification under Probate Code § 16061.7, which typically concerns informing beneficiaries of their rights and the existence of the trust.
  • A letter from "Nicole Patterson's" office to the recipients of the notification clarifies that while they can receive information, they are not clients of that office, which represents Nicole Patterson in her fiduciary capacity (likely as a trustee). This suggests potential adversarial relationships or at least a need for independent counsel for the beneficiaries.
