Frequently Asked Questions: The Fernandez Family Trust

1. What is the primary purpose of The Fernandez Family Trust as outlined in this document?

The primary purpose of The Fernandez Family Trust is to hold in trust the community property and separate property of the Settlors (Husband and Wife), manage it for their benefit during their joint and respective lives, and provide for their issue (currently two named children) upon the death of the last survivor.

2. What types of property can be held within The Fernandez Family Trust, and how is the character of the property maintained after transfer to the trust?

The trust is designed to hold various types of property, including community property, separate property, and quasi-community property. Importantly, the document specifies that the character of the property (whether separate or community/quasi-community) is retained even after its transfer into the trust. Joint tenancy property can also become part of the trust estate.

3. Who are the current beneficiaries of The Fernandez Family Trust, and how are they mentioned in the document?

The document explicitly names two living children of the Husband and Wife as beneficiaries: Frank G. Fernandez, Jr., born January 20, 1952, and James R. Fernandez, born October 6, 1953. The trust is intended to benefit them after the Settlors' deaths.

4. What broad powers are granted to the Trustees in managing The Fernandez Family Trust assets?

The Trustees are granted extensive powers to manage the trust assets, essentially possessing the rights, powers, and privileges of an absolute owner, subject to their fiduciary duties and limitations within the trust document. These powers include broad investment authority under the prudent person rule, the ability to invest in various securities (including market and index funds, options, puts, and calls, and margin accounts), retain any property (even unproductive or underproductive with the surviving spouse's consent), manage and hold securities in various forms, acquire, dispose of, manage, and encumber property, lease property (including for oil and mineral development), and more.

5. Can the Settlors (Husband and Wife) modify or revoke The Fernandez Family Trust during their joint lifetimes? If so, are there any restrictions?

Yes, the Settlors can amend, revoke, or terminate the trust or any part of it during their joint lifetimes. However, this power can only be exercised if the distribution of the Settlor's estate during their lifetime or after death is not substantially altered, and if the action does not result in adverse tax consequences or disqualify any marital deduction sought by either Settlor's estate. Unilateral action by either Settlor to amend, revoke, or terminate their separate property portion of the trust is also permitted under certain conditions.

6. What provisions are in place regarding the distribution of assets to beneficiaries, particularly concerning S corporation stock and spendthrift behavior?

The trust outlines specific rules for handling S corporation stock to ensure the trust remains an eligible shareholder under IRS regulations. This includes potentially holding such stock in separate share trusts for each beneficiary. Additionally, the trust includes a spendthrift clause, which prevents beneficiaries from voluntarily or involuntarily anticipating, assigning, encumbering, or subjecting their interest in the trust income or principal to creditors' claims or legal processes before they actually receive it.

7. What legal framework governs The Fernandez Family Trust, and where can legal proceedings related to the trust be initiated?

The validity, construction, interpretation, and administration of The Fernandez Family Trust are governed by the internal laws of the State of California, excluding its conflict-of-law rules. However, matters relating to real property within the trust are governed by the laws of the state where the real property is located. The trust document also explicitly states that Probate Code sections 17200-17210, or their successors, which authorize optional probate court jurisdiction over revocable trusts, apply to all trusts created under this instrument, making probate court jurisdiction available for trust-related matters.

8. What is the role of a forensic accountant in relation to The Fernandez Family Trust, as indicated by the Engagement Letter excerpt?

The engagement letter outlines the role of a forensic accountant hired by the Fernandez Family Trust Temporary Oversight Committee to conduct a thorough financial examination. This includes a forensic audit of the trustees' financial management, review and analysis of financial records (investment portfolio, bank statements, disbursements), verification of financial decisions aligning with trust terms, regular reporting to the committee, collaboration with legal counsel on financial and legal issues, and the preparation of a final financial audit report. The overarching goals are to ensure financial accuracy, assess the prudence of investment decisions, evaluate the fairness of asset distributions, identify any conflicts of interest or breaches of fiduciary duty, and ensure transparency in all trust-related activities, ultimately protecting the interests of the beneficiaries.
