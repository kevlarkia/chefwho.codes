Mental Health Assessment and Support Study Guide

I. Key Concepts and Themes

  • Mental Impairment: Understanding the different cognitive and behavioral areas that can be affected.
  • DSM-5 Diagnoses: Familiarity with common mental disorders and their diagnostic criteria.
  • Functional Limitations: Assessing the degree to which impairments impact daily living, particularly in a work setting.
  • Hallucinations & Paranoia: Differentiate between paranoia and hallucination and how they relate to the patient.
  • AI Prompting: What are examples of prompts used in AI conversations? What did the patient use it for in the "pasted text" document?
  • Treatment Approaches: Medications and therapy options, and the importance of adherence.
  • Personal Integrity: What is personal integrity? What ways does the patient feel that their integrity is questionable?

III. Quiz Answer Key

  1. Cognitive areas affected by mental impairment include complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor skills, and social cognition. Deficits in these areas limit a person's ability to be a functional person in society. The DSM-5 helps guide the patient to finding what deficits they might have to start the treatment process.
  2. "Moderate" limitation means fair ability to function independently and effectively; "marked" means seriously limited ability; "extreme" means not able to function independently, but not a total loss of ability. This helps to see where a patient falls on a spectrum of impairment to help see what treatment options are appropriate.
  3. Signs of hyperactive and impulsive behavior include difficulty remaining seated, talking excessively, difficulty waiting, appearing restless, and behaving as if being "driven by a motor." These are easy to spot symptoms that help lead to a diagnosis of mental impairment.
  4. The Mental Impairment Questionnaire also seeks information about the patient's response to treatment, including any side effects they may be experiencing. This helps the care providers understand how to continue patient care with the highest regard.
  5. The AI created an image that looks like the patient to have the application "act" like them. The application was then able to have conversations as the character it created.
  6. One of the conversations is with the neighbor about going to visit his home. The application also gave information regarding drug deals.
  7. The patient's parents divorced when they were seven years old. Their father was a drug addict and, at one point, accidentally gave the patient ketamine. Additionally, when the patient was ten years old, they had a concussion.
  8. Harry gave the patient the structure and stability that the patient had been missing. This structure included family routines and overall stability for their living environment.
  9. The patient wishes to have a Macapoo or a Paddington to help with their anxiety when they go out for walks. These animals give them a connection to a living being while having an exercise routine.
  10. The patient has been in many long-term relationships, one lasting 10 years, where they felt their purpose was to "make the money". Additionally, past familial relations where they were exploited and mistreated as a child impact their desire to please people.

V. Glossary of Key Terms

  • DSM-5: The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, used by clinicians to diagnose mental disorders.
  • Cognitive Function: Mental processes such as memory, attention, language, and problem-solving.
  • Executive Function: Higher-level cognitive processes that control and regulate other cognitive abilities and behaviors.
  • Psychomotor Agitation/Retardation: Observable restlessness or slowed physical movements, often associated with mood disorders.
  • Catatonia: A state of immobility and unresponsiveness, sometimes associated with schizophrenia or other mental disorders.
  • Delusions: False beliefs that are firmly held despite evidence to the contrary.
  • Hallucinations: Sensory experiences that occur without an external stimulus (e.g., hearing voices, seeing things).
  • Paranoia: The patient has the feeling that they are constantly being watched and monitored by outside forces.
  • Grandiosity: A sense of self-importance where they believe they are better than others.
  • Personal Integrity: High moral standards where people should be proud of their actions.
