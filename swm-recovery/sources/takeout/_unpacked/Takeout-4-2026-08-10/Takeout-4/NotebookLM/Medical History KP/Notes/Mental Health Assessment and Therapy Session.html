This document is a Mental Impairment Questionnaire intended for a patient's healthcare provider to complete. It aims to assess the patient's mental impairments and their impact on functioning, particularly in a work setting. Key areas covered include:

  • Patient and Provider Information: Basic details about the patient and the person completing the questionnaire (though this section is largely blank in the provided excerpt).
  • Clinical Information (Question 2): This section requests the patient's DSM-5 mental disorders/principal diagnoses and other conditions or the focus of clinical attention.
  • Treatment and Response (Question 3): Information about the patient's treatment and their response to it is requested.
  • Medications (Question 4a): A list of prescribed medications is required.
  • Symptom Checklist: A list of various mental health symptoms and behavioral patterns is provided, including:
    • Cognitive impairments in areas like complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor, or social cognition.
    • Hyperactive and impulsive behavior.
    • Delusions or hallucinations.
    • Mood disturbances (depressed mood, irritability).
    • Social interaction deficits (qualitative deficits in verbal and nonverbal communication, detachment).
    • Anxiety-related symptoms (preoccupation with serious illness, panic attacks, disproportionate fear or anxiety).
    • Behavioral issues (increase in goal-directed activity, disregard for others, restlessness, agitation, disorganized behavior, aggression, repetitive behaviors).
    • Other symptoms like sleep disturbance, pressured speech, decreased energy, appetite disturbance, and symptoms of altered motor or sensory function.
  • Intellectual Functioning (Question 8): The questionnaire asks whether the patient's intellectual functioning is limited and requests an explanation if the answer is yes.
  • Work-Related Limitations (Question 9): This crucial section asks the provider to rate the degree of the patient's expected limitations in a work setting across various functional areas using a scale of None-Mild, Moderate, Marked, and Extreme. The scale defines these levels:
    • Moderate: "the ability to function independently, appropriately, effectively, and on a sustained basis is fair"
    • Marked: "the ability to function independently, appropriately, effectively, and on a sustained basis is seriously limited"
    • Extreme: "not able to function independently, appropriately, effectively, and on a sustained basis, but it does not mean a total loss of ability to function." The specific areas for rating include:
    • Understanding information
    • Remembering information
    • Applying information
    • Interacting with others
    • Concentrating
    • Persisting
  • Mental Status Examinations (Questions 17 & 18): The form inquires whether the patient has had a CDR (Clinical Dementia Rating) or MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination) and requests the results if so.
  • Earliest Date of Applicability (Question 19): The provider is asked to specify the earliest date the described symptoms and limitations apply.
  • Comments (Question 20): A section for any additional relevant comments.
