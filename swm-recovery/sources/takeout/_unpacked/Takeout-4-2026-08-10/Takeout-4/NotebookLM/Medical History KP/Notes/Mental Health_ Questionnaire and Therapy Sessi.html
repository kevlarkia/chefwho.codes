The sources provide information related to mental health through a Mental Impairment Questionnaire and transcripts of therapy sessions.

Mental Impairment Questionnaire:

  • The questionnaire is designed to assess a patient's mental health and work-related limitations [1, 2].
  • It gathers patient details such as name, date of birth, and contact frequency [1, 2].
  • It covers DSM-5 diagnoses, treatment responses, medications, and side effects [1, 3].
  • The questionnaire documents clinical findings and the severity of mental impairment symptoms [1, 3].
  • It lists cognitive and behavioral symptoms like cognitive decline, hyperactivity, delusions, and mood disturbances [3, 4].
  • The questionnaire assesses intellectual functioning and work setting limitations [3, 4].
  • It evaluates limitations in understanding, remembering, applying information, and interacting with others [4, 5].
  • The questionnaire addresses the chronicity of mental disorders and the patient's ability to manage benefits [4, 5].
  • It includes questions about current or past substance abuse and its impact [2, 5].
  • The form includes areas to identify signs and symptoms such as [6-10]:
    • Significant cognitive decline
    • Hyperactive and impulsive behavior
    • Delusions or hallucinations
    • Irritability
    • Qualitative deficits in communication and social interaction
    • Preoccupation with having a serious illness
    • Increase in goal-directed activity or psychomotor agitation
    • Disregard for the rights of others
    • Depressed mood
    • Detachment from social relationships
    • Diminished interest in activities
    • Distrust and suspiciousness
    • Restlessness
    • Instability of interpersonal relationships
    • Sleep disturbance
    • Pressured speech
    • Observable psychomotor agitation or retardation
    • Preoccupation with perfectionism and orderliness
    • Decreased energy or being easily fatigued
    • Feelings of guilt or worthlessness
    • Muscle tension
    • Difficulty concentrating or thinking
    • Disorganized thinking (speech)
    • Thoughts of death or suicide
    • Excessive emotionality and attention seeking
    • Flight of ideas
    • Decreased need for sleep
    • Distractibility
    • Involvement in activities with a high probability of painful consequences
    • Somatic symptoms with excessive thoughts, feelings, or behaviors related to the symptoms
    • Grossly disorganized behavior or catatonia
    • Appetite disturbance with weight change
    • Panic attacks followed by worry about additional panic attacks
    • Symptoms of altered voluntary motor or sensory function
    • Disproportionate fear or anxiety about different situations
    • Involuntary preoccupation with intrusive, unwanted thoughts
    • Exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or violence
    • Subsequent involuntary re-experiencing of the traumatic event
    • Avoidance of external reminders of the event
    • Disturbance in mood and behavior
    • Increases in arousal and reactivity

Transcripts of Therapy Sessions:

  • The conversations reveal discussions around managing bipolar disorder, including dealing with hallucinations and paranoia [11, 12].
  • The importance of routine and structure in managing mental health is highlighted [11, 13].
  • The role of medication adherence in controlling symptoms is emphasized [12, 14].
  • The need for a strong support system and the challenges in finding one are discussed [15, 16].
  • The difficulty in finding the right therapeutic approach and dealing with changes in providers is evident [17, 18].
  • There are discussions of safety plans, coping mechanisms, and the importance of self-awareness in managing mental health crises [15, 19].
  • The impact of past trauma and the challenges of unpacking those experiences in therapy are mentioned [11, 17].
  • There is an emphasis on recognizing and addressing feelings of abandonment [17, 20].
  • There are discussions of side effects of medications, including sexual appetite and sleep disturbances [11].
  • There is discussion of substance use and its impact on mental health [13].
  • One individual notes a history of concussions and fractures, as well as hospitalizations for mental breakdowns [21].
  • One individual states that there is a family history of Huntington's disease [22].
  • There are discussions about managing feelings of paranoia and strategies used to reality-test [11, 23].
  • One individual states, "The definition of crazy is doing the same thing over and over again expecting different results" [24].
  • There are discussions around triggers for certain people with bipolar disorder [15].
  • One individual states that they have a "bag of tricks" to help them cope which includes hand cream, chapstick, crystals and goofy hats [15].
  • There are several instances where the individual in the sessions has had difficulty finding the right medication and care options with their insurance [18].
  • One individual shares that having a structured environment is important to maintaining stability [13].
