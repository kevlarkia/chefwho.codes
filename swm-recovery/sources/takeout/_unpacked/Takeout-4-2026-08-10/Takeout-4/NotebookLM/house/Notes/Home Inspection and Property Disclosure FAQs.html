Here's an 8-question FAQ based on the provided sources:

  • What is a limited visual home inspection, and what does it cover? A limited visual inspection, as performed by companies like Buy Right Home Inspections, is a non-destructive evaluation of the readily accessible systems and components of a home. The purpose is to identify any items that may need immediate major repair, but it's not a guarantee or insurance policy. The inspection does not include areas that are concealed or inaccessible.

  • What are some common items explicitly excluded from a standard home inspection? Typical exclusions encompass items like private water/sewage systems, saunas/steam baths, radio-controlled devices, furnace heat exchangers, freestanding appliances, security alarms, geological stability, termite infestations, environmental hazards (asbestos, radon), building value appraisals, and underground piping for pools/spas.

  • If purchasing property in a trust, what disclosures are required? If property is held in a trust, the trustee must complete a TDS and other disclosures that would be required of other owners if (i) any such trustee is a natural person AND (ii) the trust is a revocable trust, AND (iii) the trustee either is the former owner of Property or was an occupant in possession of Property within the preceding year.

  • What should buyers know about defensible space and fire hardening? Buyers should determine if the property is in an area with a local vegetation management ordinance. Sellers may be required to provide documentation of compliance before closing. Buyers should also consider potential flood zones and the availability of flood insurance.

  • What are some environmental hazards buyers should be aware of, and what experts should they consult? Potential environmental hazards include mold, lead-based paint, underground storage tanks, contaminated soil/water, and electromagnetic fields. Buyers are advised to have the property inspected and consult with appropriate experts during the inspection contingency period. Older homes built before 1935 may have underground storage tanks.

  • What are some factors buyers should consider when assessing a property's suitability for their intended use? Buyers should consider neighborhood conditions (schools, crime), noise sources (airports, traffic), zoning ordinances, short-term rental restrictions, and the availability/quality of communication services. Checking with local government agencies and third-party professionals is recommended to verify legal requirements for changes in use.

  • What do buyers need to know about solar panel leases? Tax credits, incentives, renewable energy credits, green tags, carbon offset credits, utility rebates or any other non-power attributes of the system are the property of and for the benefit of the solar company, usable at its sole discretion. You cannot sublease, assign, sell, pledge or in any other way transfer your interest in the system or the solar lease without prior written consent.

  • What are some issues related to property boundaries, easements, and potential encroachments? Fences, hedges, and walls may not accurately reflect legal property boundaries. Existing structures might not comply with setback requirements or may encroach on neighboring properties. Buyers should consider hiring a licensed surveyor to determine lot dimensions, boundary locations, and acreage.
