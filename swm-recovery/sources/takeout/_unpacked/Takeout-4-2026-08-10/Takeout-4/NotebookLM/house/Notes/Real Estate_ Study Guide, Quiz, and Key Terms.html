Navigating Real Estate: A Comprehensive Study Guide

I. Quiz

Answer the following questions in 2-3 sentences each.

  1. What are the key factors that a Trustee must disclose when selling a property held in a revocable trust?
  2. Explain the significance of a liquidated damages clause in a real estate agreement, especially in relation to properties with 1-4 units intended for owner occupancy.
  3. Describe defensible space and outline the different responsibilities a buyer or seller may have regarding local vegetation management ordinances.
  4. Why is it important for a buyer to independently investigate property boundaries and potential encroachments?
  5. What are some of the environmental hazards that buyers and sellers should be aware of and why is it important to get the property inspected for these hazards?
  6. Explain the potential issues associated with septic systems and wells on a property and why independent evaluations are recommended.
  7. What are Mello-Roos Community Facilities districts, and why should buyers be aware of them?
  8. What are the potential impacts of being in proximity to high-speed rail, airports, or areas with noise disturbances?
  9. What is the significance of the Statewide Buyer and Seller Advisory (SBSA) and what are its key functions in a real estate transaction?
  10. In the context of solar panel leases, what benefits does SolarCity retain and how does this affect a homeowner?

II. Quiz Answer Key

  1. A Trustee must complete a TDS and other disclosures if they are a natural person, the trust is revocable, and the Trustee was the former owner or occupant of the property within the preceding year. This ensures transparency and informs the buyer about the property's condition and history.
  2. A liquidated damages clause allows the seller to retain the buyer's initial deposit if the buyer breaches the agreement, serving as predetermined compensation for the seller's losses. In properties with 1-4 units intended for owner occupancy, California law limits the deposit subject to liquidated damages to 3% of the purchase price, offering some protection to buyers.
  3. Defensible space refers to measures taken to protect a property from wildfires by managing vegetation. Depending on local ordinances, either the buyer or the seller may be responsible for obtaining documentation of compliance, with the seller often obligated to comply before closing if the sale triggers the requirement.
  4. Fences, hedges, and walls may not align with legally-defined property boundaries, and existing structures may violate local setback requirements, making it crucial for buyers to verify these details. Retaining a licensed surveyor is the only reliable way to confirm lot dimensions, boundary locations, and acreage, which can impact the buyer's purchasing decision.
  5. Environmental hazards, including mold, lead-based paint, and underground storage tanks, can adversely affect the health of individuals living on or working at the property. Inspections are vital because these hazards are not always apparent and can lead to significant remediation costs and legal liabilities.
  6. Septic systems may have been abandoned or leaked, and wells can have water quality and quantity issues, even with neighboring properties connected to the sewer line. Independent evaluations by qualified professionals are recommended to assess the condition, capacity, and future suitability of these systems, as well as to test the water quality.
  7. Mello-Roos Community Facilities districts allow for the financing of public services and infrastructure through special taxes levied on properties within the district. Buyers should be aware of Mello-Roos districts because these additional taxes can significantly impact the overall cost of owning the property.
  8. Proximity to high-speed rail, airports, or areas with noise disturbances can affect the property's value and the residents' quality of life due to noise, traffic, and potential health concerns. Buyers should independently assess noise levels, traffic patterns, and potential disruptions before committing to purchase a property.
  9. The Statewide Buyer and Seller Advisory (SBSA) is a comprehensive document that informs buyers and sellers about a wide range of potential issues and risks associated with real estate transactions. It serves as a disclaimer, advising parties to seek expert advice and conduct independent investigations on various aspects of the property and its surroundings.
  10. SolarCity retains ownership of all tax credits, incentives, renewable energy credits, and other benefits associated with the solar panel system. This arrangement grants SolarCity the exclusive right to claim and use these benefits, and the homeowner is obligated to cooperate to facilitate these claims, even if future benefits arise.

IV. Glossary of Key Terms

  • Trustee: An individual or entity that holds and manages property or assets for the benefit of another party (the beneficiary).
  • Revocable Trust: A trust that can be modified or terminated by the grantor (the person who created the trust) during their lifetime.
  • TDS (Transfer Disclosure Statement): A form used in California real estate transactions where sellers must disclose material facts about the property that could affect its value or desirability.
  • Liquidated Damages Clause: A provision in a contract that specifies the amount of money one party will pay to the other in the event of a breach of contract.
  • Defensible Space: An area around a structure that is cleared of flammable vegetation to reduce the risk of wildfire spreading to the property.
  • Encroachment: A situation where a structure or improvement extends beyond the property line onto neighboring land.
  • Environmental Hazards: Substances or conditions that can negatively affect the environment and human health, including mold, lead-based paint, asbestos, and underground storage tanks.
  • Septic System: An on-site wastewater treatment system that typically consists of a septic tank and a leach field, used in areas not connected to a public sewer system.
  • Well: A hole drilled or dug into the earth to access groundwater for drinking, irrigation, or other purposes.
  • Mello-Roos Community Facilities District: A special tax district in California that allows local governments to finance public services and infrastructure, with the cost passed on to property owners within the district.
  • Statewide Buyer and Seller Advisory (SBSA): A comprehensive advisory document provided in California real estate transactions that informs buyers and sellers about various potential issues, risks, and conditions related to the property and transaction.
  • Net Metering: An arrangement with a utility company that allows homeowners with solar panels to receive credit for excess electricity generated and sent back to the grid.
  • Fixture Filing: A legal document that indicates personal property is so connected to real property that it becomes a fixture and thus part of the real property.
  • Solar Lease: An agreement in which a homeowner leases solar panels from a third-party company, rather than purchasing them outright. The homeowner pays a monthly fee for the use of the system.
  • Home Inspection Generalist: A home inspector who provides a broad overview of the condition of a property, but does not act as an expert in any particular craft or trade.
  • Advisory: Guidance in the contract suggesting clients seek expert advice.
  • Homeowners Association (HOA): An organization in a subdivision, planned community, or condominium building that makes and enforces rules for the properties and their residents.
  • Megan’s Law: Legislation in California that provides public access to information about registered sex offenders.
  • Easement: A right to cross or otherwise use someone else's land for a specified purpose.
  • Setback Requirement: The minimum distance a building or structure must be set back from a street or property line.
  • Culvert: A structure allowing water to flow under a road or railway.
  • Underground Storage Tank (UST): A tank located beneath the surface of the ground used to store petroleum or other regulated substances.
