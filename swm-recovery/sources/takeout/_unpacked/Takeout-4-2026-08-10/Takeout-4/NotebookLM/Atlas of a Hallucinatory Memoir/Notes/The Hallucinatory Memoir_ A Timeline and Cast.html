Detailed Timeline of Events

This timeline focuses on the events directly described or clearly implied in the provided excerpts from "The Hallucinatory Memoir." It is presented chronologically based on the narrative flow and mentions of time.

  • Childhood:

    • Experiences what are described as potentially traumatic childhood memories involving a house with yellow shag carpet and black and white checkered walls, and an incident with "Charlie" and a whip involving the narrator's mother. These memories are questioned by others as possibly being the narrator's "overactive imagination."
    • Recalls claiming to own a hot air balloon and a Mercedes as a child.
    • Grows up in a chaotic environment with a biological father described as a "trust fund hillbilly" with drug issues.
    • Remembers earliest childhood memory of father teaching how to test cocaine purity at age five.
    • Lives in a Midwestern state, around marijuana, a motorcycle club, and drug processing.
    • Extended family life described as extreme with "catastrophic" dysfunctions and many sad events. Aunt is married high up in a motorcycle club.
    • Experiences a period of unconditional love and lack of boundaries from their grandfather, starting from a young age. Grandfather is a stable and predictable figure who spoils the narrator.
    • Grandfather builds the narrator a treehouse for their eighth birthday as a "place of his own."
    • Experiences the death of their uncle Victor Dominic in his 30s, which deeply affected their grandfather.
    • Experiences maternal gaslighting, which contributes to a foundation of distrust.

  • Adolescence/Early Adulthood:

    • Develops the tendency to steal things compulsively without needing them, followed by guilt and disposal of the items.
    • Experiences the onset of mental health challenges, including shadows in peripheral vision and voices.
    • Starts seeking professional help and is "started on the pills" (psychiatric medication). The shadows become quieter but do not fully disappear.
    • Forms a close relationship with their grandfather, considering him a "real anchor" until his death at age 25.

  • Recent Past/Present (Implied to be ongoing):

    • Develops a strong reliance on routine, even bad routines, for structure.
    • Engages in internal "voice chat" that often leads to impulsive actions like shoplifting.
    • Experiences intense paranoia, such as the belief that strangers are photographing them.
    • Has been attending therapy sessions "religiously for months" and discusses experiences like tracking software and shadow people. Questions the reality of the therapist.
    • Makes "progress" in therapy, despite doubts about the therapist's existence, working on issues like integrity and self-control.
    • Develops a "photographic memory (Of Things That Didn't Happen)," taking photos to verify the existence of things they might hallucinate.
    • Deals with chronic physical pain from a fused ankle and back pain.
    • Is on a "legally prescribed cornucopia of psychotropics" at high doses of Seroquel, leading to concern from new doctors.
    • Recalls a journey to find the right medication cocktail, including misdiagnoses (depression, anxiety, incorrect attention deficit), ineffective first-line treatments, worsening second-line treatments, and bizarre side effects from experimental combinations (metal taste, musical dreams, inability to cry).
    • Maintains a detailed medicine cabinet.
    • Experiences seasonal fluctuations in sexual appetite, with increased libido in the spring, leading to "questionable decisions" on dating apps.
    • Notes that antipsychotics can drastically affect libido, swinging between low and high.
    • Has difficulty with judgment in romantic relationships due to an "unreliable brain."
    • Recalls a date where they were convinced the person was wearing a wire.
    • Describes the challenges of dating with mental illness, including paranoia, questioning connections, and deciding when to disclose their experiences.
    • Remembers a period of hypomania where they created a "sex spreadsheet."
    • Develops strategies to "convince people you're normal," including observing others, practicing expressions, using stock phrases, and creating socially acceptable coping mechanisms. Acknowledges these methods often fail.
    • Reflects on the "Diagnosis Carousel," receiving multiple psychiatric labels (delusions with paranoid features, anxiety disorder with obsessive features, sensory processing issues, sleep disorder).
    • Describes the "medication merry-go-round" and various side effects experienced from different psychotropic drugs (gynaecomastia from Risperdal, tasting colors from an antidepressant, memory gaps from an anti-anxiety medication).
    • Encounters Marco, who shows genuine interest, leading to the narrator's suspicion and attempts to test reality (cilantro test).
    • Collects "evidence" of interactions with Marco (saved texts, photos, recordings) due to paranoia.
    • Experiences a "breaking point" in the potential relationship with Marco upon finding a rare edition of "The Glass Menagerie" in his apartment.
    • Mentions a new psychiatrist last year who commented on the substantial dose of Seroquel.
    • Has not had a drink since 2018.
    • Sister is aware of some of the narrator's struggles and routines.
    • Friend Rachel observed the narrator seeming "present," followed by the narrator ghosting her.
    • Current state involves ongoing struggles with hallucinations, paranoia, self-sabotage, and navigating mental health treatment and social interactions.

Cast of Characters

  • The Narrator: The protagonist and author of the "hallucinatory memoir." Experiences a reality frequently at odds with conventional perception, marked by hallucinations (shadows, voices), paranoia, compulsive behaviors (like shoplifting), and self-sabotage. Relies on routine for stability and has been in therapy and on various psychiatric medications. Possesses a dark sense of humor and self-awareness, often questioning their own sanity and the nature of reality.
  • The Shadows: Hallucinatory entities that the narrator perceives in their peripheral vision. They often feel like they are watching or conveying negative messages.
  • The Voices: Auditory hallucinations that tell the narrator they are doing things wrong or prompt impulsive actions.
  • The Therapist (Potentially Non-Existent): A compassionate professional to whom the narrator has been baring their soul for months, discussing their unusual experiences. The narrator frequently questions whether this therapist is real or a figment of their imagination.
  • Marco: An individual who shows genuine interest in the narrator, leading to suspicion and paranoia. He is described as a graphic designer working on a novel. He remembers details about the narrator, triggering their distrust, and shares a similar taste in literature (Tennessee Williams).
  • Charlie: A figure associated with a traumatic childhood memory involving a whip and the narrator's mother in a strange house.
  • The Narrator's Mother: Involved in the traumatic childhood memory with "Charlie." Described as someone who engaged in maternal gaslighting, contributing to the narrator's distrust. In later years, settled down with Michael William but drinks "too much." The narrator feels they have to "deal with" her belief that she is "dying every day."
  • The Narrator's Grandfather: A stable and predictable figure in the narrator's childhood, described as their "real anchor." He was unconditionally loving and indulged the narrator, building them a treehouse and ensuring they had everything they wanted. His death at age 25 (for the narrator) was a significant loss. He lost his own son, Victor Dominic.
  • Victor Dominic: The narrator's uncle, who died in his 30s. His death deeply affected the narrator's grandfather.
  • The Narrator's Biological Father (The "Trust Fund Hillbilly"): Addicted to drugs and financially irresponsible. Family lore suggests he once accidentally gave the narrator ketamine as an infant. Taught the narrator how to test cocaine purity at age five.
  • The Narrator's Aunt: Married high up in a notorious motorcycle club. Part of the narrator's extended family described as having extreme and catastrophic dysfunctions.
  • Michael William (The Narrator's "Real Dad"): The person who provided structure and stability in the narrator's life. He saw the narrator's chaos and built boundaries to protect them. He eventually settled down with the narrator's mother. He himself experienced bipolar disorder ("was fucking bipolar then").
  • The Narrator's Sister: Appears concerned for the narrator, as seen when she finds them in the same position for eighteen hours surrounded by electronics. Receives explanations about the importance of routine.
  • Rachel (Friend): A friend who noted the narrator seeming "present," only to be subsequently ghosted. Attended a mutual friend's dinner party where she confronted the narrator about the ghosting.
  • Dr. Levine: The narrator's therapist (if real), who discussed the narrator's hypomanic phases.
  • The New Psychiatrist: A doctor who recently reviewed the narrator's medical records and expressed concern about the high dose of Seroquel.
  • Jason and Rachel (Dates during Hypomania): Examples of individuals the narrator dated during hypomanic phases, illustrating poor judgment during these times. Jason talked about cryptocurrency and borrowed the narrator's credit card. Rachel seemed unstable but appealing during an upswing.
  • The Woman from the Restaurant: A date who left abruptly and never returned, leaving the narrator unsure of the reason.
  • The Date Who Learned About the Sex Spreadsheet: A person the narrator dated who reacted with humor to the disclosure of the sex spreadsheet and became a friend.
