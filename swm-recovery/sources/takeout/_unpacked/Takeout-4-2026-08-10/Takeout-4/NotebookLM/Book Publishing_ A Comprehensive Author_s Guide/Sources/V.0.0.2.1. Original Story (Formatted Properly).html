1. Original Story (Formatted Properly)

Fake It Till You Break It

I. The Confession

The whiskey burned just enough to remind me I was still here.

We were gathered in what could loosely be called a living room , though it had long since given up on looking like one. The couch had permanent indentations, the coffee table was buried under a mix of takeout containers and unopened mail , and the entire space smelled vaguely of beer, stale popcorn, and existential dread .

Josh sat on the couch, spine straighter than his moral compass, scrolling his phone like a social scientist studying human dysfunction—except this was just gossip, and he was eating it up like a starving man at an all-you-can-drama buffet.

Heather was in the fridge, not so much looking for food as searching for a new meaning to life .

Steve was sprawled on the floor, beer balanced on his stomach, somewhere between awake and dead .

And me? I was in the corner, whiskey in hand, feeling like an actor who forgot their lines mid-performance .

The movie on the screen wasn’t important. It was just background noise to our collective unraveling.

Then, before I could stop myself, the words slipped out.

“I’ve been lying.”

Josh barely looked up. “About what?”

I swirled the ice in my glass, delaying the inevitable.

“I’m faking it.”

That got his attention. “You’re faking it?”

Steve grunted from the floor. “Faking what? Your job? Your life? Your ability to drink whiskey without looking like you’re questioning your entire existence?”

“All of it,” I admitted, taking another sip.

A pause. An actual silence , which in this group was unnatural.

Then, right on cue, Heather bulldozed through the moment like a drunk freight train .

“She had a turkey.”

II. The Turkey Incident

Josh blinked. “Who had a turkey?”

“My cousin. At her baby shower. A real one. Just… walking around. Like a guest.”

I rubbed my temples. “Why?”

Heather took a slow, dramatic sip from a half-empty bottle of rosé.

“That’s the wrong question. The right question is: why the hell was it wearing a diaper?”

Josh looked like he wanted to shut down his entire brain. “Why does your life always sound like a deleted scene from a Coen Brothers movie?”

Heather shrugged. “It just does.”

She flopped onto the couch, entirely unbothered by my soul-baring moment .

“Anyway, back to you, oh Great Pretender. What exactly are you faking?”

III. The Spiral of Truths

I exhaled. “You really want the list?”

Josh gestured lazily. “Oh, please. Enlighten us.”

“Fine. I’m faking my enthusiasm for my job. I’m faking my interest in small talk. I’m faking the ability to function like a normal adult when, in reality, I want to lie on the floor and let life happen without me participating.”

Josh took a long sip of his drink. “You need serious help.”

“You need serious help,” I shot back, because immaturity is my default defense mechanism .

Steve, suddenly invested, sat up. “Okay, but seriously—why was the turkey there?”

Heather nodded, pointing at him with the rosé bottle. “Finally. Someone asking the right questions.”

IV. The Monster & The Breakdown

Just as we were about to spiral deeper, Heather’s eyes went wide .

“Steve. Get the fucking monster.”

Josh sat up straight. “What monster?”

Steve, always eager for a fight that wasn’t necessary , jumped to his feet. “Where?”

Heather pointed under the table.

I sighed. “If this is another turkey, I swear to—”

And then we saw it.

Emerging from under the table , dragging a stolen slice of pizza like a seasoned criminal making off with a priceless artifact, was a raccoon —the real MVP of the night, effortlessly outclassing all of us in the fine art of survival.

For a moment, no one moved.

Then:

• Josh screamed like he was witnessing a murder.

• Steve lunged at it, fully believing in his own abilities.

• Heather threw the rosé bottle, missing spectacularly.

And me? I just sat back and let it happen .

Because at this point, the raccoon had its life together more than I did .

It locked eyes with me , pizza hanging from its mouth, and I swear, in that moment, we understood each other.

“I get it, buddy,” I murmured. “We’re all just trying to survive.”

The raccoon took one last triumphant bite, then bolted out the back door .

Steve slammed it shut behind him, panting.

“What the actual fuck was that?”

Heather, still staring at the door, shook her head. “That was… symbolic.”

Josh rubbed a hand down his face, like a man who had aged five years in the last five minutes. “You people are why therapy exists.”

V. The Morning After

The next morning, we sat in the kitchen like survivors of a truly idiotic battle—one fought with whiskey, questionable decision-making, and a raccoon that had more ambition than the rest of us combined.

Heather had a coffee and a hangover .

Steve was googling “do raccoons carry emotional baggage?”

Josh was staring into the abyss , trying to process all of it.

Finally, he broke the silence.

“So. Are you gonna keep faking it?”

I stared at the table, at the spot where the raccoon had been, wondering if it had a more solid five-year plan than I did.

“Probably.”

Josh nodded, sipping his coffee like a disappointed life coach. “Cool. Just let me know when you decide to stop. Not holding my breath, though.”
